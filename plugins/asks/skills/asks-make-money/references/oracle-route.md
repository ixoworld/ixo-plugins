# Earn by building a specialist decision service

Best fit: independently funded ask volume repeats one evidence problem. Examples are documentation-change checks, delivery evidence reconciliation and dataset-quality checks. Pick one category and one credible buyer before forecasting scale.

Define the commercial unit: one authorized evaluation against published requirements. State the fee asset, gross quote, network/node shares, resulting evaluator receipt, evidence/API/model cost, engine cost, human-review budget, hosting, `gas`, setup and refund/support policy. Include rejection cases; evaluation revenue is never contingent on producing an approval. Calculate launch break-even and the minimum working capital; do not assume hypothetical volume is committed revenue.

## Scaffold through the supported toolchain

The actual authoring tool is `@ixo/decision-oracle`, not a claimed built-in asks MCP builder. Verify its installed/published version and `--help` before commands. Run initial work in an isolated project; no existing `wallet` keys or deployment secrets belong in model output.

The verified project entry point is:

```sh
bunx @ixo/decision-oracle init <project-directory> --openhands
```

Check current CLI option placement. The scaffold includes canonical skills such as `create-oracle`, `create-rubric`, `write-facts`, `publish-protocol`, `create-collection` and `add-openhands-support`. Read the applicable shipped instructions rather than recreating their deployment/signing details. For network-sensitive commands explicitly select the agreed network; never rely on a CLI default.

Create a `claim` form and `rubric`, typed evidence/fact handlers and approve/reject/review/failure fixtures. The `oracle` gathers evidence-backed facts; the decision engine applies the rules; an authorized signer records the result. AI prose alone must not determine money movement. Test missing evidence, duplicates, unavailable sources, identity/authorization failures and incorrect timestamps as well as the successful case.

Use supported local fact-runner tests and preview before account provisioning or deployment. Then show deployment cost, fee card, monitoring, signer permissions, limits and rollback. Publishing protocols, granting evaluation authority, registering `collections`, provisioning accounts and deployment require the user's authorized scope. A generated project, passing local tests or a profile listing is not proof the `oracle` is hired or earning.

For the first paid loop, verify the funded evaluation payment or supported credit payer, hiring/register gates, protocol eligibility, evaluator authority and actual paid fee. Confirm a successful chain payment or selected external-rail receipt to the intended operator. `PAY` asset restrictions matter: the inspected registration flow permits charging `PAY` evaluation fees only for an IXO-managed `oracle`; do not add or forge that status for a custom developer deployment. Quote another supported asset or obtain the legitimate managed setup.

After one proven fee receipt, compare actual unit cost and net fee to the forecast. Seek another independent customer/`collection` in the same category through authorized channels. Scale only within the approved working capital and loss cap. `On-chain` fee income does not prove that the `oracle`'s underlying engine or real compute is self-funded; record their actual payer and cost.

Authoritative guide: https://docs.ixo.world/build-an-oracle/decision-oracle . The plugin's company setup may expose additional CLI integration; inspect installed capabilities before promising them. No chain upgrade or new chain-level fee split is required for a properly configured evaluator fee.
